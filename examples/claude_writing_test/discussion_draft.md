# Discussion

This study applied a DEG-based systematic screening strategy to identify candidate prognostic biomarkers in laryngeal cancer. By establishing a candidate gene pool through differential expression analysis and subsequently evaluating prognostic associations through univariate Cox regression, we identified a subset of genes that may be associated with patient survival outcomes. This two-stage screening approach enabled the prioritization of differentially expressed genes for their potential prognostic significance.

The analytical pathway employed in this study—differential expression analysis followed by univariate Cox regression—represents a systematic approach to candidate biomarker discovery. By first identifying genes with altered expression in tumor samples, we established a biologically relevant candidate pool that reflects transcriptional changes associated with the disease phenotype. The subsequent application of survival analysis to this candidate pool enabled the identification of genes whose expression patterns may be associated with patient prognosis. This sequential screening strategy has been applied in transcriptomic biomarker studies [ref], where the integration of expression profiling with clinical outcome data helps prioritize genes for further investigation.

The DEG-to-survival analysis pathway offers several advantages for candidate prognostic gene discovery. First, restricting survival analysis to differentially expressed genes reduces the multiple testing burden compared to genome-wide survival screening, while maintaining biological relevance by focusing on genes with demonstrated expression changes in the disease context. Second, this approach enables the identification of both risk and protective factors, as genes may exhibit either positive or negative associations with survival outcomes. The systematic nature of this screening method allows for unbiased candidate identification across the transcriptome, which may reveal genes not previously implicated in disease prognosis. However, the candidates identified through this approach require subsequent validation to establish their utility in prognostic assessment.

Several limitations should be acknowledged. First, this study was based on retrospective analysis of public datasets, which may introduce biases related to patient selection, data collection protocols, and cohort characteristics. Second, the findings require validation in independent cohorts to assess their reproducibility and generalizability across different patient populations and clinical settings. Third, the lack of experimental validation limits our ability to establish functional relationships between the identified genes and disease progression or patient outcomes. Despite these limitations, this systematic screening approach identified candidate prognostic biomarkers in laryngeal cancer through differential expression and Cox regression analysis, providing candidates that warrant further investigation in independent validation studies.
