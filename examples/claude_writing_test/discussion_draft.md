In this study, we identified a five-gene prognostic signature for laryngeal cancer through differential expression analysis combined with Cox regression modeling using TCGA data. Our analysis revealed genes with heterogeneous hazard ratios, including both risk-associated and protective factors, reflecting the multifactorial nature of cancer prognosis. The identification of genes with diverse prognostic effects suggests that laryngeal cancer outcomes are influenced by complex biological processes rather than single molecular pathways. This multi-gene signature approach captures the biological heterogeneity inherent in tumor progression and patient survival. The integration of transcriptomic profiling with survival analysis enabled the construction of a prognostic model that may contribute to risk stratification in laryngeal cancer patients. These findings demonstrate that systematic analysis of gene expression patterns in relation to clinical outcomes can identify molecular features associated with disease prognosis.

Our DEG-based Cox regression approach aligns with established methodologies for prognostic signature development across multiple cancer types, as similar workflows combining differential expression analysis with survival modeling have successfully identified biomarker panels in glioblastoma, gastric cancer, and other malignancies [PMID:36733371; PMID:32791689; PMID:31416476]. The identification of genes with mixed hazard profiles is consistent with previous studies demonstrating that prognostic signatures often comprise both risk and protective factors, reflecting the complex interplay of molecular mechanisms underlying tumor biology [PMID:36733371; PMID:32085563]. This heterogeneous pattern of prognostic associations has been observed in immune-related gene signatures and functional biomarker panels, where opposing effects contribute to overall predictive performance [PMID:39667460; PMID:36424614]. Our findings extend the application of multi-gene prognostic models to laryngeal cancer, contributing to the growing body of literature demonstrating that integrated transcriptomic-clinical analyses can identify molecular signatures associated with patient outcomes [PMID:38817665; PMID:36733371]. The consistency of our methodological approach with prior studies in head and neck cancers and other malignancies supports the validity of our analytical framework [PMID:32791689; PMID:36424614]. Furthermore, the identification of a compact gene signature aligns with previous work showing that relatively small gene panels can achieve meaningful prognostic stratification when derived through rigorous feature selection methods [PMID:36733371; PMID:31416476].

The methodological strengths of our study lie in the systematic integration of differential expression analysis with survival modeling, a validated strategy for biomarker discovery that has demonstrated clinical utility across various cancer types [PMID:36733371; PMID:32791689; PMID:36424614]. The use of Cox regression for prognostic gene identification represents a widely adopted computational approach in cancer genomics, enabling the derivation of multi-gene signatures that account for time-to-event data and censoring patterns inherent in survival analysis [PMID:36733371]. Our workflow mirrors successful applications in glioblastoma and gastric cancer research, where similar analytical pipelines identified four to five gene prognostic models with demonstrated predictive capacity [PMID:36733371; PMID:32791689; PMID:31416476]. The utilization of TCGA data provides a robust foundation for biomarker discovery, as this resource offers comprehensive molecular profiling with associated clinical annotations that enable rigorous survival analyses [PMID:36733371; PMID:36424614]. The identification of genes with diverse hazard ratios through our approach is consistent with the biological reality that cancer prognosis reflects multiple cellular processes and genetic contributions, rather than uniform directional effects [PMID:36424614; PMID:39667460]. This methodological framework has been validated through multiple independent studies demonstrating that integrating transcriptomic data with clinical outcomes can successfully identify clinically relevant prognostic signatures [PMID:36733371; PMID:32791689; PMID:36424614].

Despite these strengths, several limitations warrant consideration when interpreting our findings. First, our analysis was retrospective and based exclusively on publicly available TCGA data, which may limit the generalizability of our prognostic signature to other patient populations with different demographic characteristics, treatment protocols, or disease stages. Second, the absence of independent cohort validation represents a significant limitation, as external validation in separate datasets is essential to confirm the reproducibility and clinical utility of prognostic biomarkers before potential clinical implementation. Third, our study lacks experimental validation of the identified genes, and the biological mechanisms underlying their prognostic associations remain to be elucidated through functional studies. The reliance on computational analysis without laboratory confirmation means that the causal relationships between gene expression patterns and clinical outcomes cannot be definitively established. Future research should prioritize validation of this gene signature in independent laryngeal cancer cohorts to assess its robustness across different clinical settings and patient populations. Additionally, experimental investigations examining the functional roles of these genes in laryngeal cancer biology may provide mechanistic insights that could inform therapeutic strategies. Prospective studies incorporating this prognostic signature into clinical decision-making frameworks would be valuable to determine whether molecular risk stratification can improve patient management and outcomes in laryngeal cancer.
