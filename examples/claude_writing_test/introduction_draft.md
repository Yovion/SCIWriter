Laryngeal cancer represents a significant clinical challenge among head and neck malignancies, characterized by substantial disease burden and poor patient outcomes, particularly in advanced-stage presentations. As one of the most prevalent malignancies affecting the upper aerodigestive tract, laryngeal squamous cell carcinoma accounts for a considerable proportion of cancer-related mortality worldwide, with five-year survival rates remaining disappointingly low despite advances in multimodal treatment approaches [PMID:40239580; PMID:41234877]. The disease typically manifests with obstructive symptoms that often delay diagnosis until advanced stages, when therapeutic options become limited and prognosis significantly worsens. Unlike other head and neck cancers, laryngeal squamous cell carcinoma is typically unrelated to human papilloma virus infection, suggesting distinct etiological pathways that may influence disease progression and treatment response [PMID:40239580]. The aggressive nature of this malignancy, combined with its tendency for local invasion and regional metastasis, underscores the urgent need for improved prognostic stratification tools that can guide personalized treatment decisions and identify patients at highest risk for disease progression. Current clinical staging systems, while useful, often fail to capture the biological heterogeneity of laryngeal cancer, highlighting the necessity for molecular biomarkers that can provide more accurate prognostic information and facilitate early intervention strategies.

Recent molecular profiling studies have identified several genetic and epigenetic alterations that may serve as prognostic biomarkers in laryngeal cancer, though significant limitations remain in their clinical implementation. Recurrent mutations in key tumor suppressor genes and oncogenic pathways, including TP53, CDKN2A, PIK3CA, and NOTCH1, have been documented as important participants in laryngeal cancer development, suggesting their potential utility in risk stratification [PMID:39452555; PMID:41234877]. Epigenetic modifications, particularly N6-methyladenosine (m6A) RNA methylation and histone lactylation, have emerged as important regulatory mechanisms that influence gene expression patterns, tumor metabolism, and the composition of the tumor microenvironment in laryngeal cancer [PMID:40229748; PMID:40642601]. Additionally, immune-related gene expression profiles and characteristics of the tumor immune microenvironment have been associated with clinical outcomes in laryngeal squamous cell carcinoma patients, with particularly strong associations observed in smoking-related cases [PMID:35910213]. Non-coding RNAs, including circular RNAs, have also been identified as important regulators of gene expression and cellular processes in laryngeal cancer, representing potential diagnostic and prognostic markers [PMID:39153653]. Despite these advances, the clinical utility of individual biomarkers remains limited due to the complex and heterogeneous nature of laryngeal cancer biology. Most identified biomarkers have been evaluated in relatively small patient cohorts, and their independent prognostic value beyond conventional clinicopathological parameters remains uncertain. Furthermore, the mechanisms through which these molecular alterations influence disease progression and treatment response are incompletely understood, limiting their translation into clinical practice.

Transcriptome-based approaches have provided valuable insights into the molecular landscape of laryngeal cancer and offer promising avenues for comprehensive biomarker discovery. High-throughput sequencing technologies enable systematic profiling of gene expression patterns across large patient cohorts, facilitating the identification of transcriptomic signatures associated with clinical outcomes. These approaches have successfully identified key molecular alterations in laryngeal squamous cell carcinoma, including mutations and expression changes in critical regulatory genes that participate in disease development and progression [PMID:39452555; PMID:40239580]. Transcriptomic analyses have also revealed the importance of epigenetic regulatory mechanisms, such as RNA methylation and histone modifications, in modulating gene expression programs that influence tumor behavior and patient prognosis. The ability to simultaneously assess thousands of genes provides opportunities to develop multi-gene prognostic signatures that may capture the biological complexity of laryngeal cancer more effectively than single biomarkers. Moreover, transcriptome-based studies can identify previously unrecognized gene expression patterns and regulatory networks that may represent important determinants of disease progression and therapeutic response. Integration of transcriptomic data with clinical and pathological information enables the development of comprehensive prognostic models that account for both molecular and clinical disease characteristics, potentially improving risk stratification accuracy.

Despite advances in understanding the molecular basis of laryngeal cancer, significant gaps remain in our knowledge of prognostic biomarkers and their clinical applications. While various molecular alterations have been identified as participants in disease development, the prognostic biomarkers and mechanisms of progression in laryngeal cancer remain poorly understood, limiting our ability to accurately predict patient outcomes and guide treatment decisions [PMID:41234877]. Most existing studies have focused on individual genes or limited gene sets, and comprehensive transcriptome-wide screening efforts to systematically identify prognostic biomarkers are lacking. Furthermore, the independent prognostic value of transcriptomic biomarkers beyond established clinical and pathological parameters has not been thoroughly evaluated in large, well-characterized patient cohorts. The heterogeneity of laryngeal cancer, both at the molecular and clinical levels, necessitates the identification of robust biomarkers that can reliably stratify patients across diverse populations and treatment settings. To address these gaps, the present study aims to conduct systematic transcriptome-based screening to identify gene expression biomarkers associated with prognosis in laryngeal cancer patients. Specifically, we seek to identify differentially expressed genes that correlate with clinical outcomes, evaluate their independent prognostic value in multivariable analyses, and assess their potential utility for risk stratification in clinical practice. Through comprehensive transcriptomic profiling and rigorous statistical validation, this study may contribute to improved prognostic assessment and personalized management strategies for patients with laryngeal cancer.
