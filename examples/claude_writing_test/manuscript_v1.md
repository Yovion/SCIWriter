# Title

Identification of Prognostic Biomarkers in Laryngeal Cancer Through Differential Expression and Cox Regression Analysis

# Abstract

**Background:** Identifying prognostic biomarkers is essential for understanding patient survival outcomes in cancer. This study aimed to identify genes associated with prognosis through a systematic transcriptomic screening approach.

**Methods:** Differential expression analysis was performed using the edgeR package to identify genes with altered expression between tumor and normal samples. Subsequently, univariate Cox regression analysis was conducted to evaluate the prognostic significance of the differentially expressed genes using survival analysis methods.

**Results:** A total of 4,774 genes were identified as differentially expressed, with 2,797 genes upregulated and 1,977 genes downregulated in tumor samples. Univariate Cox regression analysis identified 16 genes with significant associations with patient prognosis, including 9 risk factors and 7 protective factors. Representative genes included B4GALNT2, BCAT1, CHST11, DHPS, and DIO1.

**Conclusion:** This systematic screening approach identified candidate prognostic genes that warrant further investigation as potential biomarkers for patient survival outcomes.

# Introduction

Laryngeal cancer represents a significant clinical challenge among head and neck malignancies, characterized by substantial disease burden and poor patient outcomes, particularly in advanced-stage presentations. As one of the most prevalent malignancies affecting the upper aerodigestive tract, laryngeal squamous cell carcinoma accounts for a considerable proportion of cancer-related mortality worldwide, with five-year survival rates remaining disappointingly low despite advances in multimodal treatment approaches [PMID:40239580; PMID:41234877]. The disease typically manifests with obstructive symptoms that often delay diagnosis until advanced stages, when therapeutic options become limited and prognosis significantly worsens. Unlike other head and neck cancers, laryngeal squamous cell carcinoma is typically unrelated to human papilloma virus infection, suggesting distinct etiological pathways that may influence disease progression and treatment response [PMID:40239580]. The aggressive nature of this malignancy, combined with its tendency for local invasion and regional metastasis, underscores the urgent need for improved prognostic stratification tools that can guide personalized treatment decisions and identify patients at highest risk for disease progression. Current clinical staging systems, while useful, often fail to capture the biological heterogeneity of laryngeal cancer, highlighting the necessity for molecular biomarkers that can provide more accurate prognostic information and facilitate early intervention strategies.

Recent molecular profiling studies have identified several genetic and epigenetic alterations that may serve as prognostic biomarkers in laryngeal cancer, though significant limitations remain in their clinical implementation. Recurrent mutations in key tumor suppressor genes and oncogenic pathways, including TP53, CDKN2A, PIK3CA, and NOTCH1, have been documented as important participants in laryngeal cancer development, suggesting their potential utility in risk stratification [PMID:39452555; PMID:41234877]. Additionally, immune-related gene expression profiles and characteristics of the tumor immune microenvironment have been associated with clinical outcomes in laryngeal squamous cell carcinoma patients, with particularly strong associations observed in smoking-related cases [PMID:35910213]. Non-coding RNAs, including circular RNAs, have also been identified as important regulators of gene expression and cellular processes in laryngeal cancer, representing potential diagnostic and prognostic markers [PMID:39153653]. Despite these advances, the clinical utility of individual biomarkers remains limited due to the complex and heterogeneous nature of laryngeal cancer biology. Most identified biomarkers have been evaluated in relatively small patient cohorts, and their independent prognostic value beyond conventional clinicopathological parameters remains uncertain. Furthermore, the mechanisms through which these molecular alterations influence disease progression and treatment response are incompletely understood, limiting their translation into clinical practice.

# Methods

## Differential Expression Analysis

Differential expression analysis was performed to identify genes with altered expression between tumor and normal samples. The analysis was conducted using the edgeR package in R. Genes meeting the differential expression criteria were retained for subsequent prognostic screening.

## Univariate Cox Regression Analysis

To evaluate the prognostic significance of the differentially expressed genes, univariate Cox regression analysis was performed using survival analysis methods in R. Each gene was individually assessed for its association with patient survival outcomes. Genes demonstrating significant associations with prognosis were identified as candidate prognostic biomarkers for further investigation.

# Results

## Differential Expression Analysis

To establish a candidate gene pool for prognostic screening, we performed differential expression analysis between tumor and normal samples. A total of 4,774 genes were identified as differentially expressed. Among these, 2,797 genes (58.6%) were upregulated and 1,977 genes (41.4%) were downregulated in tumor samples, indicating a predominant pattern of transcriptional activation in the tumor phenotype.

## Univariate Cox Regression Analysis

To evaluate the prognostic significance of the differentially expressed genes, we conducted univariate Cox regression analysis using patient survival data. Sixteen genes demonstrated significant associations with patient prognosis. Among these prognostic genes, 9 were identified as risk factors and 7 as protective factors. Representative genes identified through this analysis included B4GALNT2, BCAT1, CHST11, DHPS, and DIO1, which warrant further investigation as potential prognostic biomarkers.

The reliance on computational analysis without laboratory confirmation means that the causal relationships between gene expression patterns and clinical outcomes cannot be definitively established.
